# How TOMO teaches a language (and why)

TOMO's language lessons are built on a short review of second-language-teaching research. The evidence is mixed in places and many
studies are small, so this is a sensible design, not a guarantee. Sources are linked at the end.

## What the research says, and what TOMO does with it

| Idea | What the research suggests | What TOMO does |
|---|---|---|
| **Balanced course** (Nation's four strands: meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, language-focused learning, fluency) | A good course gives learners listening/reading they understand, chances to speak or write, some deliberate study of the language itself, and practice using what they know faster | Every lesson has a **mini-dialogue** (input), **key phrases and one pattern** (language focus), a **"Your turn" speaking task** (output), and quizzes that bring earlier phrases back (fluency) |
| **Comprehensible input** (Krashen's i+1) | Input a little above the learner's level, understood from context, drives acquisition | The dialogue is short, at the learner's level, with pronunciation and meaning beside every line, and it plays as audio |
| **Pushed output and interaction** (Swain, Long) | Having to *produce* language shows learners the gaps in what they can say | Quiz questions are mostly **speaking**: reply to a real-life line out loud, or repeat a phrase |
| **Chunks, not just words** | Teaching whole useful phrases is linked to better speaking fluency in several (small) studies | Lessons teach 3-5 high-frequency **chunks** per lesson, each with audio |
| **Task-based, real-life goals** | Task-based teaching tends to help fluency; explicit practice tends to help accuracy | Each lesson starts with a **can-do goal** (e.g. "order a coffee") and ends with a task. A short pattern note keeps accuracy in view |
| **Shadowing** | Repeating audio right after hearing it helps rhythm, prosody and intelligibility more than individual sounds | "Repeat after me" questions, graded on a voice note |
| **Spaced retrieval and feedback** | Recalling something after a gap beats re-reading; feedback during recall helps | Each quiz revisits a phrase from **earlier lessons**; weak questions come back on later rounds |
| **Recasts** (corrective feedback) | Saying the learner's sentence back correctly is a common, mildly effective way to correct | Feedback gives your sentence **said correctly**, a natural version, and one tip |
| **Low-anxiety practice** (affective filter) | Stress blocks learning | Warm, encouraging tone; you can retry, skip, or type instead of speaking |

## How a voice answer is checked
1. The AI first **writes down what it heard without being told the target**. Told the target, it tends to "hear" it.
2. It then compares that with the target or the situation and gives feedback and a score out of 5.
3. It is an AI, so it can miss subtle tone differences, and it says so.
